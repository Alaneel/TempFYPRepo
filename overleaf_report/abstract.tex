\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Singapore's real estate market is divided into a strictly regulated public sector (Housing and Development Board (HDB)) and a private market, but relevant information is scattered across four competing portals, lacking a unified structure. This report explains how to create and set up a system that brings together data from these four portals into a single platform. It focuses on data scanning, deduplication, ranking, and search capabilities.

The system was built by a team and has strong web scraping pipelines that collect live data from big property sites like PropertyGuru, 99.co, EdgeProp, and SRX. The author personally designed and implemented the PropertyGuru scraper—the largest data source comprising over 53,000 listings—as well as the centralized aggregation and deduplication pipeline. Our algorithm for deduplicating composite keys uses a title-fallback method. It covers 95.5\% of address fields and works well with a variety of data structures. It also gets rid of duplicates on different platforms.

Based on this unified dataset, the author implemented advanced analytical capabilities and a user-friendly application suite. Eight Gradient Boosting Valuation (XGBoost) models targeting specific market segments exhibited a low mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) of 8.9\%. Integrating SHAP for interpretability, the models achieved a 70\% error reduction over the baseline. An ablation study further confirmed that the model's logic aligns with real-world market factors. Specifically, removing \texttt{property\_age} resulted in a 130\,pp increase in MAPE for HDB Sales, while Landed Sales only saw a 1.2\,pp rise. This difference aligns with Singapore's CPF lease-eligibility policy and has resulted in a unique demand-side discontinuity in the stock of older HDB flats. This highlights the rationality of this model's feature utilisation within its economic structure. Furthermore, the LLM-powered search engine achieved a 96.4\% F1 score in translating queries to database filters, with a blind evaluation (18 queries) confirming an adjusted F1 of $\sim$91\%. Three agentic enhancements were layered on top: multi-district geographic resolution, progressive filter relaxation with zero-result prevention, and LLM-generated fallback explanations. A multi-turn chat assistant rounds out the UI, providing property valuations driven by XGBoost and SHAP importance factors. An offline review of 42 interactions across three real estate segments found that 93\% of the facts were correct and 100\% of the responses were relevant. The system is built on a decoupled architecture, with a FastAPI backend for serving data to many users at once and a Next.js frontend for making the user experience more interactive. This setup turns raw, real-time market data into useful, multi-dimensional insights about real estate.
