\chapter{Introduction}

\section{Background and Motivation}

The housing industry in Singapore is distinctively composed of two components: public housing (which is heavily governed by HDB) and private housing (where anyone can purchase residential property). Approximately 80\% of residents live in public housing provided by HDB, thus making HDB an integral foundation for social stability in Singapore. Conversely, private housing provides an alternative for high-income individuals and investors \cite{tu2004dynamics}. Although the two markets exist separately within the framework of their respective regulatory regimes, there are intricate linkages between the two markets that can be described in complex and non-linear terms. Wealth accumulation, in terms of households transitioning from the public to private residential markets, is responsible for stimulating substantial flows of capital and demand between both levels \cite{sing2006price}.

Due to the advent of online real estate portals such as PropertyGuru, 99.co, and SRX in today's digital era, the volume of real estate transaction data has exploded. It includes not just transaction records but unstructured text descriptions attached to listings, metadata from listings, and visual images of those listings. However, real estate data is not centrally located, so real estate market participants currently suffer from a lack of access to complete data, causing significant \textbf{asymmetric information} to exist amongst them. Homebuyers, investors, and public policy decision makers often have difficulty understanding the entire real estate market and whether or not it is functioning properly, due to the fact that they are often limited to doing fragmented manual research and/or performing linear analyses that do not accurately disclose all of the interrelated aspects of how the dual nature of the real estate market functions.

\section{Problem Statement}

Three concrete problems motivated this project:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Data Fragmentation:} The limitations of the current market structure limit the ability to access and analyze important market intelligence across multiple platforms that compete for businesses.
    \item \textbf{Limitations of the method:} Secondly, standard econometric techniques that are widely used in data analysis (Hedonic Price Models) generally have strict reliance upon the use of structure for variable creation and on linear relationships among the variables. As a result, most of the potential value derived from unstructured data is lost when traditional econometric methods are used to analyze data. Additionally, traditional econometric modelling techniques have not been able to account for the dynamics of non-linear relationships that often occur in the market.
    \item \textbf{Search Usability:} Users currently are restricted from utilizing flexible filtering mechanisms via the portals in order to be able to express their requirements as naturally as possible.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Project Objectives}

The system that was made to solve these problems goes all the way from raw scraping to a web app that users can see. The specific goals that were met are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Unified Data Infrastructure:} Automated web scraping pipelines were designed and implemented to aggregate and harmonise listings from Singapore's major property portals into a single deduplicated repository.
    \item \textbf{Intelligent Property Valuation:} Per-segment gradient-boosted regression models (stratified by property type and transaction mode) were trained to estimate market values and provide SHAP-based attribution explanations.
    \item \textbf{Natural Language Interfaces:} A semantic search engine powered by Large Language Models (LLMs) was integrated, allowing users to discover properties using conversational queries.
    \item \textbf{Agentic Search and Conversational AI:} Three agentic behaviours (multi-district resolution, progressive filter relaxation, fallback explanations) and a multi-turn valuation chat assistant were implemented to extend the system beyond passive retrieval.
    \item \textbf{Personalised Recommendation:} A content-based recommendation engine derives user preference profiles from saved listings and ranks active listings by weighted similarity across property type, district, price range, and bedroom count.
    \item \textbf{Actionable User Interface:} A web application exposing all AI features to end-users through a listing portal with integrated valuation panel, NL search bar, chat assistant, and personalised recommendations.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Contributions}

This report documents the individual contributions of the author within a larger team project.
The project team collectively built an end-to-end platform that aggregates data from four
portals; the author's personal contributions are as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{PropertyGuru Data Scraper.}
    The author designed and implemented the PropertyGuru web scraper, the project's largest
    data source (53,352 of 53,497 total listings, 99.7\%). The scraper supports incremental
    crawling, session resilience, and full attribute extraction including price, floor area,
    tenure, built year, and agent information.

    \item \textbf{Multi-Platform Aggregation and Deduplication Pipeline.}
    An aggregation layer has been created at the aggregation level in 
    \texttt{pipeline\textbackslash aggregate.py} with the objective of combining 
    the data processed by the four team scrapers into one single database. The 
    first step is to normalize the many diverse data schemas and then establish 
    composite keys to remove duplicate property listing records as created by the 
    four team scrapers. A total of 86,321 raw property listing records have been 
    ingested from the four sites as follows: PropertyGuru -- 55,357; SRX -- 24,010; 
    99.co -- 5,344; EdgeProp -- 1,610. After removing all duplicate property 
    listing records, the aggregate records were cleansed to a total of 53,497 
    records remaining, and $95.5\%$ of property records were matched by the 
    address field. Additionally, a title fallback strategy to eliminate any of the 
    remaining duplicated property listing records remaining as a result of 
    degenerate collisions has reduced the total number of records created as 
    duplicates during this process to 0.

    \item \textbf{Per-Segment AI Valuation Models with SHAP Explanations.}
    The author built an independent model using the same 8 XGBoost models. These 8 
    XGBoost models were independently built using two different methods of 
    creating the train/test splits---using stratified sampling by property type 
    and transaction type. The authors also considered the best segment test set 
    mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) for both HDB (Housing Development Board) 
    rent and HDB sale to be $8.90\%$ and $8.96\%$ respectively, corresponding to a 
    reduction of $70\%$ of the mean of the baseline. Finally, each of these 
    predictions includes associated SHAP attribution factors and can be viewed by 
    the user.

    \item \textbf{LLM-Powered Natural Language Property Search.}
    Formal Evaluation was carried out using 47 truth queries to measure the 
    performance of the semantic search pipeline designed based on Claude. An 
    average F1 score of $96.4\%$ was achieved in this evaluation.

    \item \textbf{Agentic Search Enhancements.}
    In addition to extracting basic intent for a user query, the author has built 
    three different types of agent NLP behaviours. These behaviours are: 
    (1)~Geographic resolution across multiple boroughs, whereby all queries will 
    now return results based on the proximity of the Orchard MRT station to a 
    collection of surrounding Singapore boroughs (D9, D10, and D11) rather than 
    limiting the query result set to just one borough; (2)~Stepwise query filter 
    relaxation, whereby all queries that return zero results undergo a re-execute 
    using progressively less strict filter criteria, first by price, next by 
    property type, and last by location; and (3)~A natural language explanation 
    generated by an LLM explaining the user on why their original query returned 
    no results, as well as the filter criteria that were relaxed in order to 
    conduct the query again.

    \item \textbf{AI Valuation Chat Assistant.}
    The assistant is provided with all property attributes and XGBoost-based valuation 
    estimates (including SHAP attribution factors) as context. This allows it to 
    answer questions such as ``Is this a fair price?'' and ``What factors influence 
    this estimate?'' using property-specific data. An offline valuation system 
    encompassing 42 interactions across three real estate segments (sales of 
    condominiums, rental apartments in HDB units, and sales of single-family homes) 
    demonstrated a \textbf{factual consistency of 93\%} and a \textbf{response 
    relevance of 100\%}.

    \item \textbf{Backend API and Frontend Web Application.}
    The author built the FastAPI backend (JWT authentication, Redis caching, RESTful listing,
    valuation, chat, and recommendation endpoints) and the Next.js frontend (listing portal,
    detail page with AI valuation panel, NL search bar, chat assistant panel, Saved Listings
    page, and personalised \emph{For You} recommendations page).

    \item \textbf{Personalised Recommendation Engine.}
    The author designed and implemented a hybrid content-based and valuation-grounded
    recommendation system. A five-component weighted scoring function combines property-type
    affinity, district affinity, price-band similarity, bedroom-count proximity, and a
    \emph{bargain score}---computed in-process from the XGBoost valuation models trained in
    contribution~3---to rank active listings against each user's saved-listing preference
    profile. An offline NDCG evaluation across ten synthetic profiles yielded
    \textbf{NDCG@5 = 0.811} and \textbf{Precision@5 = 0.800}, with 100\% type-match and
    100\% district-match at rank~1.
\end{enumerate}
