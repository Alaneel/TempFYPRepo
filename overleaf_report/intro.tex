\chapter{Introduction}

\section{Background and Motivation}

The housing market in Singapore is unique in that it consists of two levels: a public housing market, which is strictly controlled by the Housing and Development Board (HDB), and a private housing market where anyone can buy a house. The HDB sector provides housing for approximately 80\% of the population and plays a crucial role as a pillar of national social stability, while the private sector targets high-income households and investors \cite{tu2004dynamics}. Although these two markets are separated by regulatory requirements, they are closely linked in complex and non-linear ways. The desire for wealth accumulation—that is, the wish of households to move from the public to the private housing market—generates a dynamic flow of capital and demand that connects the two levels \cite{sing2006price}.

The rise of online property portals like PropertyGuru, 99.co, and SRX in the digital age has led to an explosion of real estate data. This includes not only records of transactions but also unstructured text descriptions, listing metadata, and visual assets. But this information is still spread out across different platforms, which means that stakeholders don't have all the information they need—creating a significant \textbf{information asymmetry}. People who buy property, investors, and policymakers all have a hard time getting a full picture of the market. They often have to do fragmented manual research or simplified linear analyses that don't show how the dual market system works.

\section{Problem Statement}

Three concrete problems motivated this project:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Data Fragmentation:} Important market intelligence is stored on different platforms that compete with each other, which makes it hard to analyze data from different sources.
    \item \textbf{Limitations of the method:} Traditional econometric methods, like Hedonic Pricing Models, usually depend on structured variables and linear assumptions. These methods often miss out on the valuable information that can be found in unstructured data. Also, they often don't take into account how the market changes in a non-linear way.
    \item \textbf{Search Usability:} Right now, users have to deal with rigid filter interfaces on portals, which makes it hard for people who want to say what they need in a more natural way.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Project Objectives}

The system that was made to solve these problems goes all the way from raw scraping to a web app that users can see. The specific goals that were met are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Unified Data Infrastructure:} Automated web scraping pipelines were designed and implemented to aggregate and harmonise listings from Singapore's major property portals into a single deduplicated repository.
    \item \textbf{Intelligent Property Valuation:} Per-segment gradient-boosted regression models (stratified by property type and transaction mode) were trained to estimate market values and provide SHAP-based attribution explanations.
    \item \textbf{Natural Language Interfaces:} A semantic search engine powered by Large Language Models (LLMs) was integrated, allowing users to discover properties using conversational queries.
    \item \textbf{Agentic Search and Conversational AI:} Three agentic behaviours (multi-district resolution, progressive filter relaxation, fallback explanations) and a multi-turn valuation chat assistant were implemented to extend the system beyond passive retrieval.
    \item \textbf{Personalised Recommendation:} A content-based recommendation engine derives user preference profiles from saved listings and ranks active listings by weighted similarity across property type, district, price range, and bedroom count.
    \item \textbf{Actionable User Interface:} A web application exposing all AI features to end-users through a listing portal with integrated valuation panel, NL search bar, chat assistant, and personalised recommendations.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Contributions}

This report documents the individual contributions of the author within a larger team project.
The project team collectively built an end-to-end platform that aggregates data from four
portals; the author's personal contributions are as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{PropertyGuru Data Scraper.}
    The author designed and implemented the PropertyGuru web scraper, the project's largest
    data source (53,352 of 53,497 total listings, 99.7\%). The scraper supports incremental
    crawling, session resilience, and full attribute extraction including price, floor area,
    tenure, built year, and agent information.

    \item \textbf{Multi-Platform Aggregation and Deduplication Pipeline.}
    The author built a unified aggregation layer in \texttt{pipeline/aggregate.py}. This 
    system processes data from all four team-developed scrapers. It first normalizes 
    various data schemas, then applies a composite-key algorithm to remove duplicates. 
    The pipeline ingested \textbf{86,321 raw listings} from four portals. This total 
    includes 55,357 from PropertyGuru, 24,010 from SRX, 5,344 from 99.co, and 1,610 
    from EdgeProp. After processing, the pipeline produced a clean deduplicated dataset 
    of \textbf{53,497 listings}. The deduplication key achieves \textbf{95.5\% 
    address-field coverage}. A title-fallback strategy then eliminates all remaining 
    degenerate collisions. This reduced the number of collisions from 2,410 to 0.

    \item \textbf{Per-Segment AI Valuation Models with SHAP Explanations.}
    The author trained \textbf{eight independent XGBoost models} stratified by property 
    type and transaction mode. Best-segment test-set MAPE: \textbf{8.90\%} (HDB Rent) 
    and \textbf{8.96\%} (HDB Sale)---a \textbf{70\%} reduction relative to the mean 
    baseline. Each prediction is accompanied by SHAP attribution factors exposed to 
    the end user.

    \item \textbf{LLM-Powered Natural Language Property Search.}
    The author implemented a semantic search pipeline based on Claude. Formal 
    evaluations were then performed on 47 ground-truth queries. This process achieved 
    an average \textbf{F1 score of 96.4\%}.

    \item \textbf{Agentic Search Enhancements.}
    In addition to basic intention extraction, the author implemented three agent 
    behaviors based on natural language processing: (i) \textit{Geographic resolution 
    across multiple boroughs}. This associates proximity expressions, such as ``near 
    Orchard MRT station,'' with a group of neighboring Singaporean boroughs. Examples 
    include D9, D10, and D11. Instead of targeting a single borough, this broader 
    mapping improves the hit rate of location-based queries. (ii) \textit{Stepwise 
    filter relaxation}. If no results are found, queries are automatically retries by 
    progressively loosening the restrictions (price $\to$ property type $\to$ location). 
    This prevents dead ends with zero results. (iii) \textit{LLM-generated fallback 
    explanation}. This uses a second Claude call to explain in natural language why 
    the original query returned no results and which restrictions were relaxed.

    \item \textbf{AI Valuation Chat Assistant.}
    The assistant is provided with all property attributes and XGBoost-based valuation 
    estimates (including SHAP attribution factors) as context. This allows it to 
    answer questions such as ``Is this a fair price?'' and ``What factors influence 
    this estimate?'' using property-specific data. An offline valuation system 
    encompassing 42 interactions across three real estate segments (sales of 
    condominiums, rental apartments in HDB units, and sales of single-family homes) 
    demonstrated a \textbf{factual consistency of 93\%} and a \textbf{response 
    relevance of 100\%}.

    \item \textbf{Backend API and Frontend Web Application.}
    The author built the FastAPI backend (JWT authentication, Redis caching, RESTful listing,
    valuation, chat, and recommendation endpoints) and the Next.js frontend (listing portal,
    detail page with AI valuation panel, NL search bar, chat assistant panel, Saved Listings
    page, and personalised \emph{For You} recommendations page).

    \item \textbf{Personalised Recommendation Engine.}
    The author designed and implemented a hybrid content-based and valuation-grounded
    recommendation system. A five-component weighted scoring function combines property-type
    affinity, district affinity, price-band similarity, bedroom-count proximity, and a
    \emph{bargain score}---computed in-process from the XGBoost valuation models trained in
    contribution~3---to rank active listings against each user's saved-listing preference
    profile. An offline NDCG evaluation across ten synthetic profiles yielded
    \textbf{NDCG@5 = 0.811} and \textbf{Precision@5 = 0.800}, with 100\% type-match and
    100\% district-match at rank~1.
\end{enumerate}
